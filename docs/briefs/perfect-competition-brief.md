# Perfect Competition — engagement brief

## The problem
I'm a farmer on a 1.5-acre market garden with 64 beds, my own labor, and a budget for 4 temporary staff. In a few weeks I must commit the entire season's planting to some mix of tomatoes, mesclun, and carrots, and I can't undo it. The goal is to maximize profit, but I don't know prices in advance because the market sets them. If I choose wrong, I give up profit I could have earned.

## What I am assuming
- My staff will be knowledgeable and productive.
- The growing season will be fruitful.
- The market is perfectly competitive: supply and demand set prices, and I take them as given.

## Hypothesis
I expect 20 beds of carrots, 20 beds of tomatoes, and 24 beds of mesclun to maximize
season profit. Carrots have the lowest operating cost ($440 per bed; 0.833 field
hrs/wk/bed), and tomatoes have the highest revenue per bed ($8,800), assuming all
given inputs stay constant.

## How I would know I was wrong
Wrong if any other allocation of the 64 beds earns more total season profit than
20 carrot / 20 tomato / 24 mesclun, with all given inputs held the same.
