# Perfect competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant 10 tomato, 20 carrot, and 30 mesclun beds—60 of 64 available—to maximize season profit at $42,761.66 on $210,880.00 in revenue and $168,118.34 in total cost.

## Why

Tomatoes earn the highest revenue per bed ($8,800.00) yet stop at bed 10 because marginal cost versus price, not revenue per bed, governs scale. Compounding 10% diminishing returns push tomato marginal cost from $8,248.59 on bed 10 (adding $551.41) to $9,390.72 on bed 11 (destroying $590.72) (Figure 1). By contrast, only two constraints bind at the optimum—the 20-bed carrot cap and the 30-bed mesclun cap—stopping both crops while they are still profitable: relaxing the carrot cap adds $352.49 on the first extra bed ($2,094.00 price vs. $1,741.51 marginal cost) and relaxing the mesclun cap adds $246.47 on the first extra bed ($2,700.00 price vs. $2,453.53 marginal cost) (Figure 2). The 720-hour owner field pool is not a constraint on beds; it is a wage-rate breakpoint where marginal labor drops from $34.72 to $17.36 per hour. Expanding owner field hours reduces modeled profit by $17.36 per hour because a $34.72 owner hour replaces a $17.36 temp hour, though it would save $17.36 per hour in cash if the $50,000.00 owner salary is fixed. Total beds (4 slack), the tomato cap (10 slack), temp hours (1,202.78 slack), and temp workers (0.835 slack) are all slack and worth $0 to relax. Finally, although carrots and mesclun look unprofitable if each is charged the full $20,000.00 fixed cost alone, both clear average variable cost and add $36,588.89 to farm profit—dropping them would cut profit to $6,172.77.

## The judgment call

Expand carrot-capped ground first: a 21st carrot bed nets $352.49, beating a 31st mesclun bed ($246.47) by $106.02 while using one of the 4 idle beds. Pay up to $352.49 per season for the first extra carrot bed, up to $246.47 per season for the first extra mesclun bed, and $0 for general acreage or temp slots. Where the evidence ran out is outside the model: we lack lease or soil-conversion costs, the root cause of the bed caps, and whether owner pay is cash-fixed; I prioritized carrots anyway on their clear marginal-profit edge, contingent on ground costs staying below $352.49 for that first extra bed and demand holding.

## What would change my answer

A -$551.41 to +$590.72 per bed shift in tomato price, any relaxation of the carrot or mesclun caps, or a change in the $17.36 per hour temp wage.
