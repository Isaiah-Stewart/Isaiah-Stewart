# Perfect competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant 10 tomato, 20 carrot, and 30 mesclun beds—60 of 64 available—to maximize season profit at $42,761.66 on $210,880.00 in revenue and $168,118.34 in total cost.

**Why.** Tomatoes earn the highest revenue per bed ($8,800.00) yet stop at bed 10 because marginal cost versus price, not revenue per bed, governs scale. Compounding 10% diminishing returns push tomato marginal cost from $8,248.59 on bed 10 (adding +$551.41) to $9,390.72 on bed 11 (destroying -$590.72). By contrast, carrots and mesclun hit their bed caps while still profitable: relaxing the 20-bed carrot cap adds +$352.49 per bed ($2,094.00 price vs. $1,741.51 marginal cost) and relaxing the 30-bed mesclun cap adds +$246.47 per bed ($2,700.00 price vs. $2,453.53 marginal cost). The third binding constraint is the 720-hour owner field pool, which reduces modeled profit by -$17.36 per hour if expanded because the model replaces a $17.36 temp hour with a $34.72 owner hour—though it would save +$17.36 per hour in cash if the $50,000.00 owner salary is fixed. Total beds (4 slack), the tomato cap (10 slack), temp hours (1,202.78 slack), and temp workers (0.835 slack) are all slack and worth $0 to relax.

Each standalone marginal cost curve dips once when cumulative hours cross the 720-hour owner pool: tomatoes drop from $7,660.86 to $4,906.28 across beds 5 to 6, mesclun drops from $2,988.40 to $1,983.96 across beds 13 to 15, and carrots drop from $2,552.10 to $1,589.14 across beds 16 to 18. Marginal hours rise on every bed, so the wage rate halving from $34.72 to $17.36 per hour—not improving efficiency—causes the dip. This shows marginal cost measures the dollar change in total cost per added bed (hours times marginal wage plus fertilizer), not physical productivity.

Charging each crop the full $20,000.00 fixed cost standalone makes only tomatoes profitable at +$6,172.77 (AVC $6,182.72 and ATC $8,182.72 vs. $8,800.00 price), while carrots lose -$16,488.92 (AVC $1,918.45, ATC $2,918.45) and mesclun loses -$11,922.19 (AVC $2,430.74, ATC $3,097.41). That standalone loss does not matter because it triple-counts shared fixed costs and the $12,500.00 owner-tier wage premium; dropping carrots and mesclun would slash farm profit from $42,761.66 to $6,172.77 (-$36,588.89). Match each decision to its proper test: price vs. MC sets bed counts, price vs. AVC governs short-run crop inclusion (all three clear AVC and deliver $62,761.66 in total contribution), and whole-farm revenue vs. total cost and ATC ($210,880.00 vs. $168,118.34) determines long-run viability.

**The judgment call.** Expand carrot-capped ground first: a 21st carrot bed nets +$352.49, beating a 31st mesclun bed (+$246.47) by $106.02 per bed while using the 4 idle beds. Pay up to $352.49 per season for the next carrot bed, up to $246.47 per season for mesclun, and $0 for general acreage or temp slots. Where the evidence ran out is outside the model: we lack lease or soil-conversion costs, the root cause of the bed caps, and whether owner pay is cash-fixed; I prioritized carrots anyway on its clear marginal-profit edge, contingent on ground costs staying below $352.49 per bed and demand holding.

**What would change my answer.** A -$551.41 to +$590.72 per bed shift in tomato price, any relaxation of the carrot or mesclun caps, or a change in the $17.36 per hour temp wage.
