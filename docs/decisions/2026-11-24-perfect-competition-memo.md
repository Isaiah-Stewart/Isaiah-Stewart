**Recommendation.** Plant 10 tomato, 20 carrot, and 30 mesclun beds, leaving 4 of 64 beds fallow. This mix maximizes season profit at $42,761.66 on $210,880.00 revenue and $168,118.34 total cost.

**Why.** Tomatoes earn the top revenue per bed at $8,800.00, yet they stop at bed 10. Marginal cost versus price—not revenue per bed—sets optimal scale. Steep 10% diminishing returns lift tomato MC from $8,248.59 on bed 10 (+$551.41 profit) to $9,390.72 on bed 11 (-$590.72 loss). Carrots and mesclun stop at their bed caps, not on economics. A 21st carrot bed would still net +$352.49 ($2,094.00 price vs. $1,741.51 MC). A 31st mesclun bed would net +$246.47 ($2,700.00 price vs. $2,453.53 MC). Owner field hours (720/720) also bind. Expanding them lowers modeled profit by -$17.36/hr as $34.72 owner hours replace $17.36 temp hours, though cash savings reach +$17.36/hr if the $50,000.00 salary is fixed. Total beds (4 slack), the tomato cap (10 slack), temp hours (1,202.78 slack), and temp workers (0.835 slack) are all slack and worth $0.

Every standalone MC curve dips once after passing 720 owner hours. Tomatoes drop from $7,660.86 to $4,906.28 across beds 5 to 6. Mesclun drops from $2,988.40 to $1,983.96 across beds 13 to 15. This proves MC measures dollar cost per added bed, not physical productivity.

Dropping carrots and mesclun would cut farm profit by $36,588.89, down to $6,172.77. Use the right test for each choice: price vs. MC sets bed counts, price vs. AVC governs short-run crop inclusion (all three clear AVC and yield $62,761.66 total contribution), and whole-farm revenue vs. total cost/ATC ($210,880.00 vs. $168,118.34) tests long-run viability.
